Verified Learning Guide

Learn organic farming.

A practical starting plan based on guidance from India's National Centre for Organic and Natural Farming, APEDA, and the Ministry of Agriculture.

1. Understand the system

Organic farming is a whole-farm approach to soil fertility, crop diversity, and pest prevention. It is not simply stopping synthetic inputs overnight. Follow the standard that applies to your market, and do not use an organic label until its requirements are met. Organic and natural farming have related but distinct guidance and certification routes.

2. Start with your soil and field history

Get a soil test, map each plot, and record previous crops and inputs. Build a rotation that includes locally suitable legumes or green manures, recycle healthy crop residues, and use well-prepared compost where appropriate. Protect soil from erosion and avoid waterlogging. Base nutrient decisions on the test and local recommendations, not a generic online recipe.

3. Plan seed, rotation, and contamination control

Choose varieties suited to your district and market; use organic seed when available and required by your chosen standard. Rotate crops and diversify planting to interrupt pest cycles. Where neighboring farms use prohibited substances, ask your certification group or agency about buffers and drift prevention before converting a plot.

4. Prevent pests and weeds first

Use crop rotation, timely planting, resistant varieties, field sanitation, mulches, mechanical weeding, and regular scouting. Encourage beneficial organisms where practical. Use only inputs permitted by the applicable organic standard and registered for the crop and purpose. Never assume a homemade preparation or biological input is automatically approved or safe.

5. Keep records and protect harvest quality

Keep field maps, seed sources, input names and dates, compost records, harvest quantities, storage lots, and sales receipts. Store and transport organic produce separately from non-organic produce to preserve identity. These records help with farm decisions, traceability, group certification, and buyer requirements.

6. Check certification and economics early

Ask NCONF, your state agriculture department, or a recognized group/certification body which route fits your farm. APEDA administers NPOP for applicable certification, including export-oriented supply chains; PGS-India is a separate participatory route. Conversion periods, inspections, and label rules depend on the standard. Compare costs and buyers before converting large areas; yield changes and price premiums are not guaranteed.

Before changing inputs: make a plot-level plan with your local KVK. Check the official standards and scheme rules linked below. This guide is general education, not a certification decision, crop diagnosis, or fertilizer/pesticide prescription.
